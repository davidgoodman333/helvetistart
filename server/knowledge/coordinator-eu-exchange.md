# Coordinator notes — incoming EU/EFTA exchange students (Bern)

Practical guidance from an incoming-exchange coordinator. These notes describe the
usual ORDER of steps and practical tips. Official rules, deadlines and fees always
come from the official sources; if an official source says something different,
the official source wins.

## Usual order of steps

1. Find a place to stay first. Registering with the municipality and applying for
   the residence permit requires a Swiss address, so accommodation (or at least a
   confirmed address) comes before registration. Students arrange housing
   themselves; the host university points to student housing platforms.

2. Register with the municipality where you live and apply for the residence
   permit, once you have an address. EU/EFTA exchange students who live in the
   City of Bern use the City of Bern's online form for SEMP / ISEP / exchange
   students. Students living in another municipality register with that
   municipality instead.

3. Health insurance, after registering: students who stay insured in their home
   country (for example with a European Health Insurance Card) and do NOT work in
   Switzerland normally submit an application for exemption from Swiss compulsory
   health insurance to the cantonal office. Do it early after arrival; there is a
   deadline counted from taking up residence.

4. Working in Switzerland is optional. Students who plan to work should check the
   rules for working before they start a job. Taking up work generally ends the
   possibility of being exempted from Swiss health insurance, so a student who
   plans to work must not assume they will be exempt — they should check with the
   cantonal office whether they need Swiss basic health insurance instead.
   A Swiss bank account is mainly needed when you will receive a Swiss salary.

5. Get a Swiss phone number early. A local number is useful for registrations,
   landlords, the university and banks (many use SMS codes to log in). Prepaid SIM
   cards from Swiss mobile providers are the simplest option for an exchange.

## Working as a student

- EU/EFTA students may work up to 15 hours per week during the semester and
  without limit during the semester breaks (City of Bern, stay for education).
- As soon as you work in Switzerland, Swiss basic health insurance is
  compulsory, even if you have a European Health Insurance Card. Take it out
  with a Swiss health insurer within 3 months of starting work; it applies from
  your first day of work. An exemption is no longer possible.
- If you don't take out basic insurance yourself, the cantonal Social Insurance
  Office (ASV) can assign you to a health insurer. Every insurer covers the same
  basic benefits but premiums differ, so being assigned means you lose the
  chance to compare and choose a cheaper one. Joining late can also mean a
  premium surcharge and a gap in cover (ASV, basic health insurance).
- The online "notification procedure" on the SEM website is for short-term work
  by people who do NOT live in Switzerland (up to 3 months a year). It does not
  apply to students living here with a residence permit.

## Tips

- Keep digital copies of your passport/ID card, admission or exchange confirmation,
  rental contract and proof of health insurance — most steps ask for them.
- Short exchanges (up to about three months) can follow different rules from
  semester-long stays; check the official sources for your exact dates.
